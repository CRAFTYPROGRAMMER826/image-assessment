    Plain-English guide

    New to computer vision terms? Don’t fumble—we’ve got your back.

    No single number decides whether an image is good. The learned model considers these measurements together and in the context of its training examples.

    Sharpness

    Laplacian variance and Tenengrad measure how crisp edges and fine details are. Lower values often indicate blur, although intentionally smooth scenes can also be low.

    Edges and fine detail

    Edge density is how much of the image contains visible boundaries. High-frequency energy measures tiny changes; detail and noise can both raise it.

    Brightness

    Mean luminance is average brightness from 0 (black) to 255 (white). Dark or bright alone is not a fault—the model also checks whether detail has been lost.

    Clipping

    Black/white clip ratios show the share of pixels crushed to near-black or blown to near-white. High clipping means shadow or highlight detail may be unrecoverable.

    Contrast and tonal range

    RMS contrast describes the spread between light and dark pixels. Dynamic range shows how much of the 0–255 brightness scale the image uses.

    Noise

    Noise sigma estimates random grain after separating smoother content. Local variance measures small-area intensity changes, which may come from texture or noise.

    Information and gradients

    Entropy measures visual information or variety. Gradient mean/std describe how strongly and how unevenly brightness changes across the image.

    Colour

    Saturation mean/std describe colour intensity and its variation. A high value means vivid colour—not automatically oversaturation or poor quality.
